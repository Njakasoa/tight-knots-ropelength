# Physical meaning of the reviewed geometric upper bound

Theorem002 concerns the link T(M,M), which has **M separate closed components**.
It therefore bounds the total contour length of an assembly of M linked closed
filaments. It is not directly a bound for a single DNA ring or a single knotted
filament; those require knot families with one component.

If every ideal hard circular filament has radius r, uniform scaling of the
reviewed unit-radius construction gives

```
limsup_{M→∞} L_total,min(r,T(M,M)) /
             (r [M(M−1)]^(3/4)) ≤ alpha2 < 10.614.
```

Here L_total,min is the geometric infimum without an imposed container, bending
energy, friction, stretching, thermal fluctuations or excluded solvent effects.
The inequality provides configurations of sufficiently small **sufficient total
length**. It does not say that every physical assembly is shorter than that,
or that this total length is necessary.

The family lower bound with leading coefficient sqrt(8πsqrt3)≈6.598 supplies
the opposite, necessary constraint. The remaining asymptotic coefficient gap
is about4.016, a ratio below1.609 when using the certified upper endpoint.
No equality or physical free-energy optimum follows. An Euler–Bernoulli or
worm-like-chain bending term A/2 integral(kappa² ds) changes the objective and
can favor a longer geometry with gentler curvature.

The fixed endpoint10.614 is asymptotic. For a particular finite M, use an
explicit certified finite construction and its length endpoint; do not multiply
10.614 by a small crossing count. The finite-family output gives component
counts, interval lengths and the exact geometry parameters for that purpose.
